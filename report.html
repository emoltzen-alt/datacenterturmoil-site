Today's national picture. Local opposition to data centers remains broad, bipartisan, and dominated by two tools: outright rezoning denials and temporary moratoriums, both usually driven by resident concern over water use, electricity costs/grid strain, noise, and loss of rural/small-town character. Litigation is now a routine second act — developers are increasingly suing municipalities that reject or ban projects, seeking large damages under zoning and constitutional claims.

Genuinely new in the last 24 hours: the McCormick County, South Carolina Planning Commission voted July 31 to deny a 1,000-acre rezoning tied to a proposed data center and recommended both a nine-month moratorium and a protective ordinance; Forsyth County, North Carolina commissioners voted 5–2 on July 30 to reject the "Project Iron Spur" hyperscale data center rezoning near Rural Hall after six months of public fights; and national outlets amplified developer Hexa Builders' $300 million lawsuit against Monroe Township, New Jersey over its data center ban and rejection of a 1-million-square-foot project.

Rural Hall / Forsyth County, North Carolina Current

Commissioners voted 5–2 to deny a rezoning application for "Project Iron Spur," a proposed hyperscale data center campus on roughly 129 acres across from Rural Hall's town hall. The decision came after six months of deliberation, more than 400 emails, and dozens of public speakers citing environmental impact, noise, and property-value concerns; only two commissioners voted in favor.
