Today's national picture. Local pushback on data centers continues to spread beyond the traditional hotspots (Virginia, Texas) into the Midwest, South and Mid-Atlantic. Moratoriums and "study pause" ordinances remain the dominant municipal response, and residents' core concerns are consistent nationwide: water consumption, grid strain/electric rates, constant low-frequency noise, loss of rural/agricultural land, secrecy in permitting, and thin property-tax payback from incentive deals.

Genuinely new in the last 24 hours: The Indianapolis City-County Council voted 23-1 on August 10 to approve a moratorium on new data centers through the end of 2027, sending it to the Metropolitan Development Commission for final sign-off (Aug. 19). On August 11, three separate local-government proceedings on data centers are on the calendar: a Lincoln County, Nebraska Planning Commission hearing on new data-center zoning regulations (North Platte); a Windsor Township, Berks County, Pennsylvania Board of Supervisors public hearing on a zoning ordinance amendment to permit data centers; and the second of two Cumberland County, North Carolina community input sessions on a proposed data-center ordinance, held in Hope Mills.
