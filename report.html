National Trends

Local pushback against data center development kept intensifying across the country in the past day. Within a single 24-hour window, county and city bodies in California, Virginia, Ohio, Washington, South Carolina, Wisconsin, and Missouri all took up data-center fights — spanning a rejected voter-referendum proposal, a 12-month application pause in the nation's largest data-center hub (Loudoun County, VA), recall elections tied to a Meta project in Ohio, a chaotic hearing in Washington state, a newly finalized county moratorium in South Carolina, and a Conditional Use Permit hearing over a former Wisconsin papermill site.

The underlying concerns are consistent nationwide: water consumption for cooling, electricity price hikes and grid strain, constant turbine/HVAC noise, property-tax burdens shifted onto homeowners even as hyperscalers receive abatements, and skepticism that projects deliver lasting local jobs. Recent national polling puts opposition to a nearby data center at roughly 59–75% of adults, and researchers at Data Center Watch estimate more than $64 billion in U.S. data center projects have been blocked or delayed by local opposition to date.
