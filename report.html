Today's national picture. The moratorium wave keeps widening rather than slowing: county and city bodies from Colorado to Florida to New Mexico are pausing or permanently restricting new data center applications, almost always citing the same trio of concerns — water consumption, strain on the electric grid/rate impacts, and construction/operational noise. A parallel front has opened around money: several states (Illinois, Arizona, Ohio, New Jersey, North Carolina, and now Virginia) are freezing or clawing back data center tax exemptions as ratepayers connect rising bills to nearby facilities, and homeowners near operating hyperscale sites are increasingly turning to noise-nuisance lawsuits rather than just public comment.

Genuinely new in the last 24 hours: Frederick County, MD's executive froze new data center development and ordered added transparency (signed July 1, reported July 2) after 21,000 residents petitioned against a 2,600-acre overlay zone; Boulder County, CO held a July 2 public hearing on making its data-center/detention-center pause permanent; the St. Louis Board of Aldermen delayed a vote on a data center zoning bill amid disputes over whether it was rushed; three Florida "Big Bend" counties (Leon, Jackson, Wakulla) advanced or enacted bans/moratoriums, while Tallahassee's city commission voted 3–2 against a similar measure; Mount Pleasant/Sturtevant, WI homeowners filed a federal class-action suit against Microsoft's Fairwater data center over constant generator/cooling noise; developer-vs-activist "greenmail" defamation litigation over the Imperial Valley, CA data center had a court hearing in El Centro; and Clark County, NV commissioners put the county's data center application/approval criteria back on their agenda after resident pushback over a recently approved Switch expansion.
