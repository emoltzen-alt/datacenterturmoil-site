Today's national picture. Local opposition to data centers continues to spread rather than plateau: dozens of counties, cities and townships across at least a dozen states have active moratoriums, pauses, or contested rezoning fights underway. The animating concerns remain constant — water consumption, rising electricity bills, noise from cooling systems and backup turbines, loss of farmland/open space, and a widespread sense that approvals move faster than public input can keep up with. Litigation is becoming a second front: developers are now suing townships that reject or delay projects, while residents near existing facilities are suing (or petitioning) over noise and pollution from the power infrastructure that supports them.

Michigan City, Indiana Current
Common Council · moratorium ordinance tabled 8–1 · reported September 19, 2026

An effort to pause new data-center development in Michigan City stalled after the Common Council voted 8–1 to table a proposed moratorium ordinance rather than adopt it outright. Environmental advocates and residents told council members the delay "opens the floodgates" to new projects at a vulnerable moment, while the council attorney said the plan is to have the planning department and planning commission help shape any zoning-code changes before a vote.

Upper Merion Township, Pennsylvania Current
Court filing · Montgomery County Court of Common Pleas · reported September 18, 2026

Developer Brian O'Neill sued the Upper Merion Township Board of Supervisors after it unanimously rejected his five data-center site plans for a proposed 4.6-million-square-foot campus near King of Prussia. O'Neill's attorney argues the township had previously treated data centers as permitted "warehouse" uses and only reversed that position after residents pushed back on the project; supervisors say the plans lacked specificity, including on fire-safety issues.
