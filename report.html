National Trends Snapshot

The data-center backlash keeps escalating politically rather than fading: on Aug 23, Texas Gov. Greg Abbott — a governor who spent last year courting hyperscalers — said data center developers "dug their own grave" with the current backlash, days after ordering a statewide audit that has effectively frozen up to ~1,800 pending grid-connection requests. The same day, the Washington Post reported that a majority of Republican elected officials now talk about data centers negatively, and trade press described "thousands" of projects nationally as halted or delayed amid moratoriums, lawsuits and permit fights (Texas and New York's new state-level pauses chief among them).

Beneath the national headlines, the week's pattern is familiar and continues today: local boards keep splitting on moratoriums (Louisville and St. Charles passed them; Salix, Iowa voted one down), planning commissions keep recommending denial only to be overridden or contested by elected councils (Edgerton, KS; Piedmont, OK today), and noise/water lawsuits are spreading beyond the original Virginia "data center alley" hot spots into Wisconsin, Michigan, Mississippi and Tennessee. Two live votes are happening as this report is compiled: Piedmont, Oklahoma's council on "Project Open Sky," and a rescheduled Bartow County, Georgia planning hearing on a Brown Farm Road rezoning.
