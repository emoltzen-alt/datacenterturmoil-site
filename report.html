Data Center Community Response — Daily Report

Reporting window: past 24 hours, ending Aug 21, 2026 · U.S. local & state government reaction to data center development

National TrendsSummary

Local opposition to data centers keeps widening rather than settling into a few hotspots: over the past day, counties in Missouri, Wisconsin, California, and the Virginia legislature all took fresh action or held fresh hearings on data center siting, taxes, or moratoriums — on top of a background rate of roughly one new county-level moratorium vote every few days nationally.

  • Moratorium wave continues. Wisconsin's Oneida County became the latest of well over a dozen counties nationwide (recently joined by Alamance, Yakima, Calvert, Sarasota, Cass, Linn, Dodge, Calaveras, and Tulare counties, among others) to pause new data center permitting while it studies rules.
  • Money fights are catching up with siting fights. Beyond zoning, the argument is shifting to who pays: Virginia's legislature opened a formal study of the state's data-center tax exemption after a lawmaker said residents "feel steamrolled," while Colorado's Douglas County approved a $19M rebate for a 16-job facility over unanimous public objection.
  • Water and noise remain the two loudest complaints in nearly every hearing and lawsuit tracked this week, from turbine noise in Mississippi and Wisconsin to contaminated well water in Oregon and Georgia and rationed water in Western Maryland.
  • State-level responses are following local ones. Texas's marathon legislative hearing (Aug. 19, public comment window open through Aug. 21) and Gov. Abbott's earlier grid-interconnection pause show state capitals now responding to the same grievances driving county-level moratoriums.
