Local Community Response to Data Centers — Daily Report

Generated Thursday, July 2, 2026 · Coverage window: prior 24 hours (≈ July 1 – July 2, 2026), with older context where relevant.

Today's national picture. Local pushback on data centers keeps accelerating even as national construction spending hits record highs (2026 year-to-date spend is running roughly 4x the 2025 pace). The moratorium/development-pause has become the default local tool — this week alone Little Rock, Endicott (NY), and Morris County (TX) joined a growing list that already includes Minneapolis, Cleveland, Inver Grove Heights, Linn County (IA), and DeSoto County (FL) over the past month. Recurring resident concerns are consistent nationwide: water consumption for cooling, strain on the power grid and electricity bills, constant industrial-fan/HVAC noise, light pollution, loss of farmland, and questions over whether promised tax revenue offsets the costs. A parallel track has opened in the courts and at the ballot box, with referendum fights (Frederick County MD, Claremore OK, Box Elder County UT, Wrightstown WI) and noise-nuisance class actions (New Jersey, Michigan) proceeding alongside the zoning fights. Coverage this week also highlights an emerging left-right coalition of rural landowners and environmental advocates opposing projects on similar grounds.

Genuinely new in the last 24 hours: Indianapolis's Metropolitan Development Commission voted 5–3 to advance a special data-center zoning district over protester objections and calls for a moratorium; Little Rock's Board of Directors placed a hyperscale-data-center moratorium on its July 7 agenda; the Village of Endicott, NY began weighing a 12-month AI data center moratorium (vote set for July 20); Morris County, TX commissioners unanimously adopted a resolution demanding stricter state disclosure and water/power impact reviews for data center developers; and Palm Beach County, FL's Zoning Commission was set to hear the contentious "Project Tango" AI data center expansion, with a Board of County Commissioners vote to follow July 15.

Indianapolis (Marion County), Indiana Current
Metropolitan Development Commission · 5–3 vote · July 1, 2026

Dozens of protesters demonstrated outside the City-County Building before commissioners voted 5–3 to advance a new special zoning district setting rules for where data centers can be built, how close to homes, and noise limits — rebuffing residents' calls for an outright moratorium. The Little Rock Board of Directors will consider a moratorium on new large-scale ("hyperscale") data center zoning applications through January 1, 2027, at its July 7 meeting. The proposal would not affect an already-planned Google data center at the Port of Little Rock.

Morris County, Texas Current
Commissioners Court · resolution adopted, unanimous · July 1, 2026

Commissioners unanimously adopted a resolution calling on the Governor, Texas Legislature, Public Utility Commission, and ERCOT to require data center developers to disclose projected power and water use, undergo independent impact reviews, and adopt water-efficient cooling — reflecting rising concern in rural East Texas over unchecked data center growth.

Source: KLTV — "Morris County commissioners adopt resolution on data center regulation"
Western Palm Beach County (Loxahatchee), Florida Current
Zoning Commission hearing · July 2, 2026; BCC vote set July 15

The "Project Tango" AI data center campus — seeking to expand allowed development from roughly 2 million to 3.6 million square feet on a 205-acre site near a local school — heads to the county Zoning Commission Thursday. Neighbors have spent months rallying against the project over power demand and noise concerns.

Source: WLRN — "AI data center in western Palm Beach County heads to zoning hearing"
