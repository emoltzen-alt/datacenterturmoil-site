Today's national picture. The data-center backlash continues to widen and deepen: national polling puts opposition to nearby construction at roughly two-thirds of Americans, an estimated $130B+ in projects have been blocked or delayed industry-wide since the start of 2026, and the issue is increasingly reshaping local and state politics — from county-board incumbents facing recall-style pressure in VA, MD, MO, WI, MI, TX, TN and CA, to Texas Gov. Frederick County, Virginia Current
Board of Supervisors · vote (6-1) · September 24, 2026

The Board of Supervisors voted 6-1 Wednesday night to remove data centers as a permitted or conditional use countywide, making Frederick the first Virginia locality to restrict data center development this way. Two pending projects — the 220-acre Virginia Technology Park in Clear Brook and the 72-acre Winchester Gateway in Kernstown — were grandfathered but must return for a public hearing by Oct. 28.

Source: Nvdaily — "Data centers stripped as allowable use in Frederick County"
Doña Ana County, New Mexico (near Las Cruces) Current
Regulatory / corporate filing · reported September 24, 2026

Oracle sent a force-majeure notice to developer Stack Infrastructure for the $165B, 1,400-acre "Project Jupiter" data center campus (part of the Stargate/OpenAI buildout), reserving the right to delay payments if the site isn't operational by 2028. The project's natural-gas supply pipeline has stalled after New Mexico's State Land Office rejected rights-of-way, a setback tied to sustained regional opposition and scrutiny over the plan's power and water footprint near the U.S.–Mexico border.

Source: Albuquerque Journal — "Oracle invokes 'force majeure' for Project Jupiter data center in Doña Ana County"
