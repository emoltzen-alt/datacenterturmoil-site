Data Center Community Response — Daily Report

24-hour window: Sept 25, 2026 (afternoon) – Sept 26, 2026 · Compiled from public web sources and official government video

National Trends

Opposition to data-center development continued to intensify nationwide over the past day, consistent with the broader 2026 pattern: polling this month put resident opposition to new local data centers at roughly six in ten Americans, and tracking groups estimate tens of billions of dollars in projects have been blocked or delayed by local pushback so far this year. The dominant local flashpoints remain unchanged — water consumption, strain on the power grid and electricity rates, constant low-frequency noise, light pollution, and doubts about promised tax revenue and jobs.

In the last 24 hours specifically, activity skewed toward public-input and pressure-campaign events rather than final votes: a historic-site operator in Kentucky publicly broke with local officials ahead of a zoning vote, university students in Illinois rallied to toughen a draft county ordinance, Charlotte closed out a month of resident listening sessions ahead of its moratorium's expiration, and Oregon's state advisory committee opened a public-comment window on its findings. Several older, higher-stakes items — commission rejections, noise lawsuits and large rallies — remain relevant background and are listed separately below.
