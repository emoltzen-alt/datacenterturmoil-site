National Trends

  • Opposition to data centers has become a mainstream political flashpoint: a Heatmap/Embold Research survey found roughly 75% of registered U.S. voters disapprove of data center construction near their neighborhoods, while a Gallup poll found 48% "strongly oppose" one being built nearby.
  • Local restrictions are spreading fast — nearly a third of Indiana counties have adopted data center ordinances or moratoriums, New Jersey has 38+ municipal bans, and cities in Kansas (Edgerton, El Dorado, Emporia) are suing their own residents over referendum petitions aimed at blocking projects.
  • Data Center Watch estimates roughly $64–98 billion in U.S. data center projects have been blocked or delayed by local opposition, with the pace accelerating through 2026.
  • Complaints are shifting from purely water/power concerns toward noise — as more facilities come online, residents near operating sites (Michigan, Wisconsin, Mississippi) are filing lawsuits over 24/7 cooling and turbine noise.
  • Financial leverage is becoming a bargaining chip: several jurisdictions (Chesterfield County VA, Elk Grove Village IL) are using data center tax revenue to offset resident utility and car-tax bills in an effort to soften opposition.
