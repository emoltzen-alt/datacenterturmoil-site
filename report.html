National trends: Community pushback against data center construction continued to accelerate this week, with NPR and Bloomberg both reporting that the fight over AI infrastructure has become a defining local-politics issue ahead of the 2026 midterms — Data Center Watch estimates roughly $68 billion in projects have been blocked or delayed nationally, and polling this year has put public opposition to new data centers as high as 61–70%.

Three recurring flashpoints keep surfacing in this period's coverage:

  • Process and transparency — several stories (Aurora, CO; Mona, UT; Portland, OR) center on projects approved administratively or with minimal public hearing, fueling resident anger over being cut out of the decision.
  • Moratoriums as a holding pattern — counties from Baltimore, MD to Beaufort, NC to Woodbury, MN are extending or considering construction pauses while they write new rules, often before required studies are even finished.
  • Noise, water and power strain — ongoing disputes in Dowagiac, MI and Loudoun County, VA illustrate how operating facilities (not just proposed ones) are generating sustained resident complaints and litigation.
