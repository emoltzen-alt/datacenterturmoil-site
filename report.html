Today's national picture. The moratorium wave continues to be the default local response, now cutting across red and blue states alike — Minnesota, Montana, Tennessee, Georgia, Virginia, New York and Washington all saw board or council action in the last day. Recurring resident complaints remain consistent nationwide: strain on electricity rates and the grid, groundwater and aquifer depletion, constant low-frequency noise, light pollution, and skepticism that promised tax revenue and jobs offset the costs. A growing number of counties are now killing or pausing projects outright rather than just studying them.

Genuinely new in the last 24 hours: Carlton County, MN advanced an interim ordinance pausing new data centers (July 8); Missoula County, MT's Bonner Mill project was pulled entirely after public outcry over river contamination and power draw (July 8); Clark County, NV commissioners heard a Sierra Club-led push for a moratorium but took no action (July 8); Douglas County, GA commissioners voted 4-1 to deny rezoning for a 4.4-million-square-foot campus, citing strained fire/public-safety capacity (this week, confirmed July 8-9); Warren County, VA relocated its planning commission hearing to a high school gym to fit the crowd opposing a Rockland Road project (July 8); Canton, NY's town board held a hearing on a moratorium running through May 2027 (July 8); Burien, WA's planning commission took up zoning tied to its new one-year moratorium (July 8); and Shelbyville, TN's city council passed its own data center moratorium (July 9).
