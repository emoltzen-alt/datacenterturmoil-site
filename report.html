National Trends Summary

The dominant pattern remains local governments reaching for moratoriums as a stalling tactic while they write real zoning rules — dozens of counties and cities from North Carolina to South Dakota have paused or are actively considering pauses on new data-center applications this summer. Resident objections are consistent nationwide: rising electricity bills, water consumption in already-stressed basins, low-frequency noise from cooling and generators, construction damage, and skepticism that promised tax revenue offsets the costs.

Outcomes are split roughly evenly: some boards are rejecting or pausing projects outright (Imperial County CA, Naperville IL, La Pine OR, Kansas City MO), while others are approving projects or voting down proposed moratoriums despite packed, hostile hearings (Henderson NV, Westlake TX, Colorado Springs CO). A newer thread is litigation — developers suing for water rights after promising not to need it, and residents suing over construction damage or environmental-review shortcuts.

In the past 24 hours specifically, activity concentrated in North Carolina's Piedmont Triad region, where three separate local governments (Alamance County, Greensboro, and Yadkin County) held public hearings on data-center moratoriums on the same evening — a sign the regional moratorium wave triggered by Surry County's two-year pause in July is still spreading county to county.
