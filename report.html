Data Center Community Response — Daily Report

24-hour window: July 9–10, 2026 (America-wide) · Older/background items included for context

National Trends

Local resistance to data-center development continued to intensify nationwide. In the past 24 hours, elected bodies in Georgia (Marietta), Texas (Fort Worth), Montana (Missoula County) and Arkansas (Union County) all took formal action — moratoriums, permit denials, or zoning pauses — in direct response to organized community pushback, while Indiana (Posey County) and Missouri (Festus, St. Louis) saw fresh public hearings and closed-door developer presentations draw criticism.

The dominant resident concerns remain consistent across regions: water consumption and aquifer strain, electricity-rate increases passed to ratepayers, constant industrial noise from cooling systems and diesel generators, loss of farmland/rural character, and skepticism that promised tax revenue and jobs offset the local costs. A growing number of jurisdictions are opting for temporary moratoriums (Marietta GA, Missoula County MT, Union County AR, Nottingham NH) to buy time for zoning studies rather than approving or rejecting projects outright.

Virginia continues to be the epicenter of the largest fights — Prince William County's unanimous rejection of the ~2,000-acre "Dulles South" campus this week follows the earlier collapse of the Prince William Digital Gateway project, while Culpeper and Botetourt counties remain contested. Legal challenges are also emerging as a secondary front, with a Wisconsin class-action noise/light-pollution lawsuit against a Microsoft facility and a Pennsylvania judge's denial of challenges to an East Whiteland project.
