National Trends

The regulatory backlash against AI data centers kept accelerating this cycle. In the past 24 hours, five jurisdictions had live action: a ~100-person rally at Philadelphia City Hall demanding a moratorium before two proposed sites break ground; Orange County, FL (Orlando) commissioners weighing a one-year AI data center pause over a projected 96-million-gallon-per-day groundwater shortfall by 2045; Raritan Township, NJ holding a public hearing on a revised data-center ban ordinance; Piqua, OH commissioners considering recall elections and a charter amendment to ban data centers and crypto mining outright; and Beaufort County, NC formally postponing its own moratorium vote to October 5 for more public input.

Zooming out, Ohio alone now has data-center questions on the November ballot in 18 communities, and the pattern nationally is consistent: counties and townships in Illinois, Virginia, South Carolina, Georgia, Wyoming, Oklahoma and Texas are denying rezonings, drafting moratoriums, or fielding lawsuits over water use, electricity demand, noise, and property-tax abatements tied to hyperscale AI campuses. Planning commissions are increasingly siding with residents even when developers have local political support, and a growing number of towns (Grayslake/Lake County IL, Hanover County VA, McCormick County SC) are seeing planner recommendations overridden or litigated after the fact.
