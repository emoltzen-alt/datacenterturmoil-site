Today's national picture. Opposition to new data center development continues to be one of the most active local-government fights in the country, and it is no longer isolated to a handful of "hot" metros — it is spreading to rural counties (Warrick County, IN; Lincoln County, NE; Broken Bow, NE) writing zoning rules for the first time, often preemptively, before any specific project has been proposed. The dominant pattern remains the same: residents show up in large numbers to demand a full moratorium; local boards more often respond with a temporary pause (90–180 days) paired with a study period, rather than an outright ban or approval.

Warrick County, Indiana Current
Board of Commissioners · vote · August 11, 2026

Commissioners unanimously approved a 180-day deferral ordinance pausing all new data center development countywide — including the roughly 1,200-acre "Project Zora" site on reclaimed mine land in northern Warrick County — to give staff time to research and draft permanent regulations. Residents who spoke at the meeting welcomed the pause but remained wary of a data center in a rural area.

Source: WFIE 14 News — "Warrick Commissioners vote on moratorium to pause data center development"
Lincoln County (North Platte), Nebraska Current
Planning Commission · public hearing · August 11, 2026

The Planning Commission held a lengthy public hearing on nine pages of draft data center zoning rules — covering site design, utilities, environmental review, and decommissioning. Of 20 residents who spoke, only one supported the proposed regulations; commissioners recommended the rules to the full County Board, which is tentatively set to take them up August 31.

Source: The North Platte Bulletin — "After lengthy public hearing, data center regs recommended"
St. Louis County, Missouri Current
Planning Commission · public hearing · August 10–11, 2026

Nearly 20 residents spoke at a public hearing on proposed data center zoning rules for unincorporated parts of the county, with most calling for a full moratorium and citing utility-rate increases, water and energy consumption, noise, and infrastructure strain — even though no specific data center project is currently proposed there.

Source: St. Louis Public Radio — "'We don't want them': St. Louis County residents push back on zoning rules for data center projects"
Broken Bow, Nebraska Current
City Council · public hearing / first reading · August 11, 2026

The City Council held a public hearing and completed the first reading of Ordinance 1317, described locally as one of the most comprehensive data center regulatory packages proposed in Nebraska so far — addressing noise, water conservation, cooling systems, residential setbacks, financial transparency, and required FBI background checks for owners holding a 10%+ stake. Council declined to waive the standard three-reading process, extending the timeline for public input.

Source: Custer County Chief — "Council tackles full agenda including Flock Cameras and Data Centers"

Tricia Byrnes, to avoid competing with a resident rally scheduled the same day. Grassroots groups No MO Dirty Data Centers and the Missouri Rural Crisis Center will instead hold a rally in the Capitol rotunda for residents to testify directly to lawmakers about data center impacts.
