National Trends

Local resistance to data center development continued to intensify nationwide. In the past 24 hours alone, six distinct communities across five states — Louisiana, North Carolina, West Virginia (two counties), Ohio, and Kansas — held hearings or votes touched off by resident concern over water use, electricity rates, noise, and loss of local control. Durham County, NC became at least the fifth North Carolina jurisdiction in August to adopt a data center moratorium, following Indianapolis/Marion County, Alamance County, and Greensboro. Kansas' Emporia and West Virginia's Putnam County show a growing secondary pattern: citizens turning to ballot petitions and formal resolutions when councils and commissions won't act. Zoom out over the past several weeks and the background list below shows the same fights recurring in dozens of counties and cities — hearings, outright rejections, moratoriums, and a rising wave of nuisance and environmental lawsuits (noise, water contamination) once facilities are already built.
