National Trends Summary

The pattern from the past 24 hours matches the broader trend seen through the summer of 2026: local governments are moving faster than developers can react. Fort Worth, TX and East Lansing, MI both took council action in the last day to pause or extend pauses on data center construction, while St. Croix County, WI and Negaunee, MI opened new public hearings on data center ordinances today. Moratoriums remain the dominant tool communities are reaching for — dozens of counties and cities nationwide (Montgomery Co. MD, Indianapolis IN, Yakima Co. WA, Sarasota Co. FL, and many more) have enacted or extended one since spring 2026.

Where moratoriums aren't used, boards are increasingly voting down rezonings outright (Hanover Co. VA, Montour Co. PA, Prince William Co. VA), and residents who feel shut out of the process are turning to litigation (Guadalupe Co. TX, Spartanburg Co. SC, Lake Co. IL). Across nearly every item collected, the same concerns recur: water consumption, electricity rate increases, noise from cooling systems and backup generators, and skepticism that promised tax revenue and jobs will materialize.
