Today's national picture. Local opposition to data centers remains broad, bipartisan, and concentrated on the same core worries — water use, electricity bills, noise, and loss of rural/open character. Colorado Springs, Colorado Current
Planning Commission · vote 6-2 to deny appeals · reported July 24, 2026

After a marathon 14-hour hearing at the Ent Center for the Performing Arts, the Planning Commission voted 6-2 to reject resident appeals seeking to block the “Project Taurus” AI data center off Garden of the Gods Road. More than 100 people signed up to speak against the project versus 13 in support, citing noise, power and water use. The commission attached conditions requiring 10 years of utility-use reporting and capping usage at 50 megawatts; opponents have 10 days to challenge the decision further.

Source: Colorado Springs Gazette — "Planning Commission rejects resident appeals to stop proposed data center" · also KKTV
Charles County, Maryland Current
Board of County Commissioners · zoning plan pulled · reported July 24, 2026

Commissioners unanimously agreed to remove Zoning Text Amendment 25-187 — which would have defined and permitted data centers in certain industrial zones — after nearly a year of sustained public pushback. Instead of moving the ordinance forward, the county will wait on a state report and pursue hiring an outside consultant to help a new task force study data center rules from scratch.

Source: The Southern Maryland Chronicle — "Commissioners Pull Data Center Plan, Weigh Task Force" · also The BayNet
Edgerton, Kansas Current
