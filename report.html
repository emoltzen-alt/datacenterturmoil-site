National picture. Opposition to new data centers remains broad, bipartisan, and concentrated on a consistent set of concerns: water consumption, strain on the electric grid and rising bills, constant fan/cooling noise, and whether tax revenue promises are worth the tradeoffs. The dominant municipal response continues to be the temporary moratorium or development "pause," often paired with a study period to draft zoning and ordinance rules — though several jurisdictions (Edgerton, KS; Hill County, TX) show that councils can override commissions or reverse course under legal or political pressure.

Genuinely new in the last 24 hours: 4 items qualified — Denton, TX advanced a moratorium package; a Houston Public Media investigation found Texas regulators don't reliably track data centers' water/power use; Louisville's Planning Commission advanced a 180-day moratorium to the full Metro Council; and Virginia's State Corporation Commission ordered data centers, not ratepayers, to pay for dedicated transmission infrastructure.
