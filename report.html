Today's national picture. Local resistance to data centers continues to widen rather than settle: dozens of counties and cities across at least a dozen states are actively debating or enacting moratoriums, zoning bans, and rezoning denials. Water and power/utility-rate strain remain the top-cited fears, joined by noise (now the subject of an active class-action lawsuit in Wisconsin) and — increasingly — anger that developers are receiving large tax abatements for projects residents don't want. Gallup recently found 71% of Americans oppose a data center being built in their own area, and tracking firms estimate well over $100 billion in U.S. data center projects have now been blocked, delayed, or withdrawn amid this opposition. An ongoing U.S. heatwave is adding fresh urgency to grid- and water-strain concerns in several of today's hearings.

Genuinely new in the last 24 hours: Nashville's Metro Council held a high-turnout, ticketed public hearing tonight (July 7) on dueling data-center zoning/moratorium bills tied to a fight over a proposed facility next to the Nashville Zoo; Bell County, TX commissioners heard residents demand an end to data-center tax abatements; the Town of Clay, NY held hearings on a new law enabling large "community host agreement" payments in lieu of permit fees, tied to a proposed Micron deal; Douglas County, GA held a hearing on a 700-acre, five-building data center complex; and Indianapolis's data-center zoning ordinance was formally introduced to the City-County Council, with a public hearing set for July 13.

Metro Council · public hearing · July 7, 2026

Metro Council used a first-of-its-kind ticketed queuing system to manage overflow crowds at tonight's public hearing on two bills: BL2026-1391, which would impose strict zoning rules on data centers, and BL2026-1448, a temporary moratorium on all data-center construction in Davidson County. At Tuesday's Commissioners Court meeting, residents pressed county leaders to scrap data-center tax abatements outright, arguing incentivized projects are "already ahead" of local taxpayers. Commissioners pushed back that the abatement tool gives them leverage over how projects are built, but the county plans a public hearing and an amended abatement policy by September 8.

Clay, New York Current
Town Board · public hearings · July 7, 2026

The Town Board held two hearings on a new local law that would let large ($100M+) projects strike "Community Host Agreements" in lieu of standard permit fees, alongside a specific proposed $30 million host-agreement deal with Micron — versus an estimated $62 million the town would otherwise collect in building-permit fees. The debate follows earlier data-center-moratorium fights in the region and turns on whether such deals shortchange the community for the sake of fast-tracking a mega-project.

Source: CNY Central — "Clay board advances proposed $30M Micron agreement, sets public hearings amid debate"
Douglas County (Douglasville), Georgia Current
County public hearing · July 7, 2026, 6 p.m.

The county held a public hearing at the courthouse on a proposal by East Village Dothan, LLC to build a five-building, 700-acre-plus data center complex south of I-20 near the Liberty Road exit. Residents argue Douglas County already hosts 11 active data centers and has "taken on more than its fair share," citing water use, noise, and quality-of-life concerns; the developer projects 200–300 permanent jobs.

Source: FOX 5 Atlanta — "Douglas County data center sparks resident pushback"
Indianapolis (Marion County), Indiana Current
City-County Council · ordinance introduced · July 6, 2026

A proposed data-center zoning ordinance (special-use district SU-47) was formally introduced to the City-County Council on July 6, after the Metropolitan Development Commission voted 5-3 to advance it despite resident protests. It would set noise limits, setbacks from protected districts, decommissioning requirements, and public reporting — Indianapolis's first zoning rules written specifically for data centers. A committee public hearing is set for July 13, with a possible final vote August 10.

Source: WTHR — "Proposed Indy data center rules advance to council despite resident concerns"
