Today's national picture. Opposition to data center development remains broad, bipartisan, and heavily concentrated on water consumption, electricity-bill and grid-strain fears, noise from cooling systems and diesel generators, and the sheer scale of hyperscale campuses relative to rural and suburban infrastructure. The moratorium — typically 6 to 18 months, paired with a study period to draft zoning and siting ordinances — remains the dominant municipal response, with North Carolina alone now counting at least 14 counties and 20 municipalities with such measures in place. A growing secondary trend is developers and pro-development interests suing local governments over bans and public-notice procedures, while opposition groups are increasingly using ballot referenda and recall petitions against officials seen as too friendly to data center approvals.

Pima County, Arizona (Tucson area) Current
Pima County Health Department · study release · September 7, 2026

One year after county supervisors approved the massive "Project Blue" data center, Pima County released a health-impact study conducted with Johns Hopkins Bloomberg School of Public Health. It identifies 21 potential pathways by which large-scale data centers could worsen air quality (diesel-generator NOx and PM2.5), strain an already "extremely high risk" water supply, and raise energy costs in a county with lower-than-average household income and high exposure to extreme heat.

Source: KJZZ — "1 year after approving Project Blue, Pima County releases data center health impact study"
