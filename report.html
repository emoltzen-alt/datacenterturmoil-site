National trends: Local opposition to data center development kept intensifying across the country over the past day, spanning at least five states in this window alone. Zoning and moratorium fights remain the dominant pattern — Fairfax County, VA finally adopted new data center zoning rules (noise studies, buffers, size caps) after months of pressure, while Spokane County, WA residents organized to sustain an existing moratorium and Coweta County, GA moved a contentious rezoning hearing to a fairground to fit the crowd. Kenosha, WI continues to see sustained, multi-month turnout against a proposed Microsoft campus. A newer front is opening at the ballot box: Boulder City, NV voters will decide in November whether to open 14,000 acres to data center development, following similar referendum fights already resolved in Wisconsin (Wrightstown, Port Washington) and pending in Maryland (Frederick County). Underlying complaints are consistent nationwide: water and electricity demand, noise from cooling systems and generators, property-tax giveaways, and a sense that approvals move faster than public input.
