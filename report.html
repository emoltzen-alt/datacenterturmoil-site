National picture. Local opposition to data centers remains broad, bipartisan, and fixated on the same core fears: water withdrawals, electricity bills shifting onto residential ratepayers, constant low-frequency noise, loss of local zoning control, and property values. More than 100 active moratoriums and eight outright municipal bans are now in effect nationwide, and data-center opponents blocked or delayed roughly $130 billion in projects in Q1 2026 alone. Political fallout is spreading too — Festus, MO voters ousted every incumbent council member who backed a $6B project, and similar recall efforts are underway elsewhere.

Genuinely new in the last 24–48 hours: the biggest story is the collapse of the 2,100-acre "PW Digital Gateway" near Manassas National Battlefield in Prince William County, VA, after QTS withdrew its final court appeal on July 2–3 — a multi-year fight residents and preservationists are calling a landmark win. Also fresh: Nottingham, NH's planning board unanimously backed a 12-month data center moratorium (reported July 3); Hoffman Estates, IL's developer withdrew a contested rezoning request after weeks of packed hearings; and new political and legislative reactions to the Hudson Valley's data-center backlash surfaced in New York (July 3).

Prince William County, Virginia Current
Court filing / project collapse · July 2–3, 2026

QTS Data Centers withdrew its appeal to the Virginia Supreme Court, officially killing the 2,100-acre "PW Digital Gateway" project — once billed as the world's largest data center campus — next to Manassas National Battlefield Park. The plan died after years of litigation by homeowners, the American Battlefield Trust, and the Coalition to Protect Prince William County, following court rulings that voided the county's rezoning over defective public notice.

Source: Potomac Local — "QTS Withdraws Supreme Court Appeal, Ending Proposed Digital Gateway Data Center Project" · also Washington Times, July 3, 2026
Nottingham, New Hampshire Current
Planning Board vote · July 1, 2026 (reported July 3)

After nearly 15,000 people signed a petition and residents packed repeated meetings over a proposed data center on Route 4, Nottingham's planning board voted unanimously to advance a 12-month construction moratorium on data centers, sending the question to a special town meeting for a resident vote.

Source: New Hampshire Public Radio — "Nottingham's 12-month moratorium on data center construction heads to a town vote"
East Fishkill / Hudson Highlands, New York Current
Political reaction / regional coverage · July 3, 2026

New coverage of the Hudson Valley's data-center backlash highlighted state Sen. Rob Rolison and Assembly Member Dana Levenberg voicing support for East Fishkill's three-year moratorium (barring permitting of any data center over 20 MW until 2029), part of what reporters describe as a fast-spreading regional and national movement of more than 100 active local moratoriums.

Source: The Highlands Current — "Data Centers Face Backlash"
Hoffman Estates, Illinois Current
Rezoning withdrawn · July 1–2, 2026

A developer withdrew its request to rezone land for a data center on the village's edge — bordering South Barrington and Barrington Hills — after weeks of organizing by neighbors. The Plan Commission had voted 4–2 to recommend denial following a three-hour public hearing that packed the room and spilled into the hallway.

Source: NPR Illinois — "Developer withdraws rezoning request for controversial data center in Hoffman Estates"
Rockford, Illinois Current
City Council · vote delayed · June 29, 2026 (reported July 2)

Residents rallied outside City Hall and filled council chambers over a proposed tax-increment finance district tied to a Monarch Energy data center site, citing fears of higher electric bills, strain on city water resources, and pollution. Aldermen delayed the TIF vote so staff can study protections used by other non-home-rule communities.

Source: St. Louis Public Radio — "Aldermen delay vote on data center zoning bill, just days before scheduled recess"
Union County, Arkansas Current
Quorum Court · emergency vote · approved late June/July 1, 2026

County leaders approved a one-year pause on new data center development (running July 10, 2026 – July 9, 2027) at an emergency Quorum Court meeting officials called the largest crowd the court has ever hosted, with every member voting for the ordinance and an emergency clause skipping the usual waiting period.

Source: Data Centers: Weekly Briefing, June 30 – July 3, 2026
Festus, Missouri Current
Developer revision · July 1, 2026

Developer CRG (Clayco) unveiled a scaled-back design for its $6 billion data center — cutting from 12 two-story buildings to four one-story buildings with larger setbacks — after sustained opposition. Residents remain skeptical, noting the plans are still labeled "conceptual"; opposition has already ousted three council members and recall petitions are circulating against the mayor and remaining council.

Source: First Alert 4 — "Festus data center developer scales back project after public opposition"
Nashville (Davidson County), Tennessee Current
Eminent domain legislation filed · June 29, 2026 (public hearing set July 7)

Mayor Freddie O'Connell filed condemnation legislation seeking to acquire ~23.5 acres near the Nashville Zoo through eminent domain to block Atlanta-based DC Blox's proposed data center, after the zoo raised concerns about harm to sound-sensitive animals and more than 500,000 people signed an opposition petition. DC Blox's attorney disputes the legal basis for the taking.

Source: Axios Nashville · WKRN
