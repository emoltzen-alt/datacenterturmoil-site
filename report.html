Roanoke County, Virginia (Wood Haven Technology Park) Current
Western Virginia Regional Industrial Facility Authority · land-purchase vote · September 11, 2026

A regional authority representing six Roanoke Valley localities voted Friday to enter a purchase agreement with a company called Clearview Dynamics for land at Wood Haven Technology Park. The authority would not confirm the intended use, but roughly two dozen opponents packed the meeting room convinced it is a data center, criticizing the lack of transparency and raising concerns about water use, strain on the electrical grid, and proximity to a residential community.

Source: WVTF — "Data center opponents sound the alarm about Roanoke Valley board vote"
Ferguson, Missouri Current
City Council · special meeting, first reading · September 11, 2026

A revived $1.8 billion tax-abatement package — with a $750,000 annual community "sweetener" — for a data center proposed at the former Emerson Electric campus returned to the council for a first reading. The meeting devolved into open conflict after the mayor restricted public comment time; residents shouted at city leaders over noise, water, electricity demand, and diesel-generator emissions. Activists have launched a recall petition against the three council members who backed the deal in May. A final vote is expected September 22.

Source: Hoodline — "Ferguson Data Center Tax Deal Sparks Council Uproar"
Charlotte, North Carolina Current
City-hosted public meeting · listening session · September 10, 2026

The city held the second of six community meetings scheduled through September — this one at the One Water Facility — for residents to question officials about water and power demands from data centers, as Charlotte's 150-day development moratorium approaches its expiration and the city weighs new policy.

Source: WBTV — "Charlotte hosting several public data center meetings in September"

Nottingham, New Hampshire Older
Town vote · approved · reported September 9, 2026

Nottingham voters approved a 12-month moratorium on any data center development within town limits by a landslide, 1,335 to 143 — a sign that opposition to data centers now extends well beyond big metro areas into small New England towns.

Source: New Hampshire Public Radio — "Nottingham voters overwhelmingly approve moratorium on data centers"
Pinal County, Arizona (near Eloy) Older
Board of Supervisors · rejected 4–1 · vote August 26, reported September 8, 2026

Supervisors rejected Vermaland's "La Osa Energy Center" data center campus even after the developer scaled it back from 59 buildings and two gas plants to 11 buildings and one plant and pledged reduced water use. Roughly 300 people attended the hearing and nearly three hours of public testimony cited water, air quality, noise, wildlife, and property-value concerns.

Source: AZFamily — "Pinal County rejects data center despite developer's concessions"
Levy County, Florida Older
Board of County Commissioners · moratorium approved · reported September 10, 2026

Commissioners unanimously approved a three-year moratorium on new data centers and other large, power-hungry facilities in unincorporated areas, halting new applications, permits, and rezonings through September 30, 2027 while the county studies infrastructure and environmental impacts.

Source: Strisker — "Data Centers: Daily Notes, September 10, 2026"

Boulder City, Nevada Older
Federal administrative ruling · stay granted · September 1, 2026

A federal administrative judge sided with the Sierra Club Toiyabe Chapter and the Center for Biological Diversity, halting what would have been the first data center on U.S. public land. The ruling found the Bureau of Land Management improperly reused an environmental review written for a solar farm to justify a 167-megawatt data center — more than triple Boulder City's own peak power demand.

Source: Boulder City Review — "Judge halts Boulder City data center on public land"

Emporia, El Dorado & Edgerton, Kansas Older
Municipal lawsuits vs. citizen petitions · background

Three Kansas cities — Edgerton, El Dorado, and Emporia — have each sued their own residents, challenging citizen petitions that sought to restrict or ban large-scale data centers. The fight has become as much about how much power voters have to overrule local officials as about data centers themselves.

Source: Newsweek — "Americans Fighting Data Centers Face Lawsuits From Local Communities"
