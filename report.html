National trends

Opposition to AI data-center development is intensifying and spreading geographically, and is increasingly bipartisan heading into the November midterms. Data Center Watch estimates roughly $64B+ in projects have been blocked or delayed by local opposition, with over 300 U.S. cities, towns and counties having adopted some form of ban or moratorium. A March 2026 Gallup poll found 70% of respondents oppose new AI data centers in their neighborhood.

  • Moratoriums are the default local response — county and city boards from Iowa to Alabama to Kentucky are passing (or narrowly rejecting) temporary bans while they write zoning rules.
  • Litigation is escalating on both sides — residents are suing to block projects (Illinois, Utah), while developers and even municipalities are suing to overturn bans and citizen petitions (New Jersey, Kansas).
  • Recurring resident concerns: electricity rate hikes, groundwater/well depletion, constant low-frequency noise/hum, property values, and skepticism about tax-incentive deals negotiated behind closed doors.
  • Politics: recall campaigns against officials who approved projects are underway in multiple states (e.g., Missouri, Texas), and data centers are shaping primary and midterm races.
