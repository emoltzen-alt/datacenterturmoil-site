National Trends

The pattern holding through mid-2026 continued today: local governments, not federal or state regulators, remain the primary battleground over data center growth. In the past 24 hours alone, seven distinct communities across six states — Indiana (two counties), Massachusetts, Alabama, Georgia, Nevada, and Illinois — held hearings, committee sessions, or public gatherings driven by resident concern over water use, electricity demand, noise, property values, and farmland loss. Momentum continues to favor opponents: Indianapolis's council committee advanced a data center moratorium 10-3 despite the mayor's public backing of the industry, and Massachusetts communities (Westfield, Holyoke, Lowell) keep adding to a growing list of moratoriums and outright bans in the Pioneer Valley and Merrimack Valley. Nationally, polling continues to show roughly 70-75% of Americans oppose siting a data center near their home, and outlets tracking the industry (Data Center Watch) estimate tens of billions of dollars in projects delayed or blocked by local opposition so far in 2026. Rejections and moratoriums still outnumber approvals in the day's news, though several counties (Maricopa AZ, Box Elder UT, Oliver ND) have continued to approve projects over resident objection, underscoring how uneven the outcomes remain county to county.
