National Trends

The dominant story of the past two days is the first coordinated nationwide day of protest against data-center expansion: opponents organized by the group HumansFirst staged roughly 142 demonstrations across 42 states on Saturday, July 18, targeting existing and proposed sites over rising utility costs, water and power demand, noise, land-use change and a lack of transparency in approvals. Coverage of the action continued into Sunday, July 19, including local rallies such as one in Cambridge, Ohio. A June Reuters/Ipsos poll found only about a third of Americans approve of the current pace of data-center construction, and just 14% would welcome one in their own community.

Beneath that headline, the week's local pattern held: several counties and towns voted down proposed moratoriums or advanced projects despite packed, hostile hearings (Lincoln County, NE; Stokes County, NC; Indianapolis east side; Red Oak, TX; Palo, IA), while others rejected or paused rezonings and tax breaks under resident pressure (Palm Beach County/Arden, FL; New Kent County, VA; Hoffman Estates, IL; Fauquier County, VA; Montour County, PA; Saline Township, MI). Litigation is now running in both directions — developers suing towns that said no (Urbana, OH) and residents suing after councils said yes (Festus, MO) — and referendum fights over data-center deals are working through the courts (Ashville, OH). Jersey City became one of the more aggressive municipal responses, advancing an outright ban on new data centers on industrial land.

Sources: Daily Signal, Jul 19 · Newsweek, Jul 18
