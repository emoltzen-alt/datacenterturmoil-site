Today's national picture. Local resistance to data-center development remains broad and bipartisan, with moratoriums and use-permit restrictions still the dominant municipal response while a handful of jurisdictions push further into utility rate reform, litigation, and — increasingly — ethics complaints against officials seen as too cozy with developers. Water use, noise, and electricity-bill impacts continue to anchor resident concerns, alongside complaints that hearings and approvals move too fast for meaningful public input.

Genuinely new in the last 24 hours: Kentucky is the day's epicenter, with three separate hearings on the same evening — a Mercer County Fiscal Court second reading on a zoning ordinance that would let a data center locate next to Shaker Village of Pleasant Hill, a Hopkinsville/Christian County public hearing on new data-center zoning standards, and a fresh report on Carroll County residents pursuing ethics complaints against officials over a data-center land deal. Elsewhere, Winneshiek County, Iowa held a hearing on a proposed moratorium; Columbus, Ohio's City Council gave a first reading to an ordinance forcing new water/power rate structures on large-load users including data centers; New Castle, Delaware's Planning Commission heard an expert briefing on data-center impacts; and Sterling Heights, Michigan hosted a public open house on its existing moratorium.
