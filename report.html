National Trends Summary

Local resistance to data center development remains intense and geographically broad. Since Amazon's abandonment of its Calvert Cliffs–area project, Calvert County, MD commissioners unanimously enacted a six-month moratorium on Aug. 18 — though residents at the hearing pushed for a full two-year pause, underscoring that even "wins" for opponents are seen as incomplete. Moratorium and ban activity continues to spread rapidly (Alamance County NC, Fort Worth TX, Marion County FL, Cullman AL, Walker County GA), while some jurisdictions are moving the opposite direction, lifting or rejecting pauses under industry and fiscal pressure (Leavenworth County KS, Stokes County NC).

Litigation is becoming a primary battleground: residents in Michigan, New Jersey, Wisconsin, Utah, and Virginia have filed or advanced lawsuits over noise, light pollution, water contamination, and land-use process, while non-disclosure agreements between developers and local officials continue to fuel bipartisan anger, most visibly in Kentucky (Boyd and Mercer counties). Several municipalities are also grappling with the flip side of the debate — how to spend windfall tax revenue from hosting hyperscale facilities (DeKalb/Aurora, IL) — showing the community response is not uniformly oppositional, even as fear of noise, water and power strain, and property-tax fights dominates most local coverage. In at least one case (Emporia, KS), tension has escalated to death threats against officials, prompting a shift to virtual, comment-free public meetings.
