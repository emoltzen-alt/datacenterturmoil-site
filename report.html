Today's national picture. Local fights over data centers continue to center on water use, electricity rates, noise, and how little public input residents feel they got before land was rezoned or permits issued. Moratoriums and permit denials remain the most common local response, but a new theme is emerging: several communities (Columbus, Georgetown/Scott County KY) are shifting from blanket bans toward drafting standing ordinances — setbacks, water-rate surcharges, decommissioning requirements — while frustration grows in places where approvals already happened with minimal notice (Mona, UT; McCormick County, SC).

City Council · special meeting/appeal vote · September 17, 2026

The City Council held a special meeting to decide two appeals against the Planning Commission's July 23 approval of "Project Taurus," a 50-megawatt Raeden data center proposed in a former semiconductor plant off Garden of the Gods Road. Washington, D.C. (federal — national context) Current
U.S. House of Representatives · bill passed 417–3 · September 16, 2026

The House passed the bipartisan Ratepayer Protection Act, requiring data centers to cover the costs of grid upgrades their power demand triggers, rather than passing those costs to ordinary ratepayers. The bill, from Rep. Gabe Evans (R-CO) and Rep. Kathy Castor (D-FL), is a direct legislative response to the wave of local anger documented in this report — a recent poll cited alongside the vote found 65% of Americans oppose a data center in their community.

Sources: Roll Call — "Bill aimed at voter anger over data centers passes House" · Washington Times
