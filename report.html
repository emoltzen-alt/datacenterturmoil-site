National Trends

Opposition to data center development continued to intensify across the country in the past 24 hours, with several jurisdictions moving from public debate into formal action. Alamance County, NC became the latest county to enact a moratorium (one year, unanimous) after a marathon public hearing, following similar recent moves in Indianapolis, Fort Worth, and Coweta County, GA. In Kansas, the fight escalated further: the city of Edgerton sued its own residents to block a citizen petition seeking to ban "high-impact" data centers — a tactic data-center opponents and now a city government are both using to route disputes through the courts. Meanwhile, hearings continued or were newly scheduled in Snohomish County, WA, Knox County, NE, and Statesboro, GA, underscoring that water use, electricity costs, noise, and loss of local control remain the dominant resident concerns nationwide. Reporting and industry trackers (e.g. Data Center Watch) estimate tens of billions of dollars in projects have been blocked or delayed by local opposition in 2026 so far.
  • Moratoriums are the tool of choice — counties and cities are increasingly pausing new approvals (Alamance, Indianapolis, Fort Worth, Coweta, Forsyth, Snohomish) rather than negotiating case-by-case.
  • Litigation is now bidirectional — residents are suing cities over blocked referendums (Independence, MO), and cities are now suing residents to block petitions (Edgerton, KS).
  • Water, power, noise, and taxes remain the consistent resident complaints, alongside frustration over secrecy in project negotiations (e.g. Effingham County, GA's Project Camellia).
