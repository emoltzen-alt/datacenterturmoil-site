National Trends

Opposition to data center development remains broad-based and bipartisan, spanning red and blue states alike. In the last 24 hours the pattern held: a Midwest city council (Elkhart, IN) moved a moratorium to a final vote; a Sioux Falls–Lincoln County, SD joint session over data-center rules turned openly contentious; Bloomington–Normal, IL held a joint public forum on regulations while both towns' moratoriums remain in effect; New Orleans' Planning Commission deferred data-center zoning rules for a second time over water-use concerns; and Wheeling, WV opponents held their own citizen-organized hearing after company and city representation was thin. Zoomed out, dozens of counties and cities nationwide have passed or are weighing moratoriums this month (Louisville, Indianapolis/Marion County, Greensboro, Lakeland, Montgomery County MD, Coweta County GA, and more), several projects have been outright rejected by councils and planning commissions, and litigation is emerging as a new front — residents and advocacy groups are increasingly suing over approval-process transparency, water and environmental impacts, and tax-abatement terms rather than only fighting rezonings at the podium.
