National Trends Summary

Local backlash against data-center development kept accelerating through late August 2026. The count of U.S. moratoriums and outright bans is already roughly 4x all of 2025's total, with New Jersey alone up to 38 municipalities that have blocked data centers by local ordinance. Resident objections center on four recurring themes: water use (especially in drought-prone regions like Imperial Valley, CA and the Southwest), electricity demand and rate hikes, 24/7 industrial noise (now the subject of multiple lawsuits in Michigan, Wisconsin and Virginia), and lopsided property-tax abatements that residents fear will shift costs onto homeowners. The fight has become explicitly political: national polling has found roughly 7 in 10 Americans oppose data centers near their communities, at least 18 officeholders have lost elections after taking a position on a local project, and a pro-AI political group launched a multimillion-dollar ad campaign this week in Kansas, Ohio and Wisconsin to push back on the backlash ahead of November's midterms.

Background sources: Data Center Watch, The Hill, Political Wire (Aug 31).
