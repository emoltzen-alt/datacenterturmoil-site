Data Center Community Response — Daily Report

24-hour window: Aug 28, 2026 12:00 PT – Aug 29, 2026 12:00 PT · Background items drawn from the prior several weeks for context

National trend: Local resistance to data center development kept accelerating through late August 2026. A Heatmap/Embold Research survey found roughly 75% of registered voters disapprove of data centers near their neighborhoods, and Data Center Watch estimates $64 billion in projects have been blocked or delayed nationwide by community opposition. The pushback is bipartisan — Republican officials in Texas and Indiana and Democratic officials in North Carolina and Missouri have each backed moratoriums in the past two weeks alone.

Moratoriums, rezoning fights and outright rejections are now landing somewhere in the country almost daily — Durham County (NC), Vermillion County (IN), Elkhart (IN), Martin County (FL) and Austin (TX) all took action in the last 72 hours. Water use, noise, electricity-grid strain and who captures the tax windfall remain the dominant resident complaints; noise has increasingly moved from complaint to lawsuit, with class actions now filed in Wisconsin and Michigan.

A newer wrinkle: courts and election boards are becoming the battleground. A California judge has tentatively set aside Imperial County's moratorium for insufficient legal justification, and an Ohio county elections board just upheld keeping a citizen-led data center ballot initiative off the November ballot — both signs that opponents' regulatory wins are now being tested in court and at the ballot-access stage.
