Today's national picture. Local opposition to data centers remains broad, bipartisan, and increasingly organized: polling this year shows roughly 63–75% of U.S. voters oppose new facilities near their communities, and opponents have blocked or delayed more than $130 billion in projects since January. Water and electricity-bill impacts, constant industrial noise, secretive non-disclosure agreements with utilities, and the sheer scale of proposed campuses are the recurring flashpoints. Moratoriums, zoning fights, planning-commission rejections (sometimes later overridden by city councils), and nuisance/environmental lawsuits are all now common municipal responses, and a national "Stop Data Centers" protest movement (including a coast-to-coast day of action in July) continues to spread.

Genuinely new in the last 24 hours: coverage is thinner than the steady drumbeat of the past few weeks, but four items are freshly reported today. In Pahrump, NV (Nye County), the Manse Technology Campus is holding a public forum tonight while residents stage a second roadside protest the same evening. Menomonie, WI's City Council is reviewing (not yet voting on) a draft I-4 zoning ordinance tightening noise, water, and setback rules for data centers. In Fairfax County/Vienna, VA, a newly filed "Vienna Cloud" data-center conversion proposal is seeking exceptions to the county's data-center rules, with state lawmakers renewing calls to pause new approvals statewide pending a water/power impact study. And Michigan outlets used today's statewide primary to spotlight continuing bipartisan backlash to data centers, a week after protesters disrupted a presidential visit in Milford over the issue.
