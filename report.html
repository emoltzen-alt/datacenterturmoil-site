Data Center Community Response — Daily Report

Window: past 24–72 hours as of September 5, 2026 (freshest verifiable coverage found ranges Sept 1–4, 2026) · National background included for context

National trends: Local pushback on data centers kept intensifying this week on three fronts. First, moratoriums remain the go-to stalling tactic — Lake County, IL is on track to approve an eight-month pause on Sept. 8 after its zoning committee's unanimous recommendation, following similar pauses already enacted in Montgomery County, MD, Indianapolis/Marion County, IN, Alamance County, NC, and Westover, AL. Second, ballot-box and recall pressure is now a real check on approvals: Trenton, OH voters will decide a data-center ban in November after the Ohio Supreme Court forced the city to certify a citizen petition, and Independence, MO voters recalled a council member specifically over his data-center tax-incentive vote — reportedly the first successful recall of its kind nationally. Third, approvals are not ending the fights — Middlesex Township, PA approved the first phase of a $15B, 700-acre campus despite a packed and hostile hearing, and Imperial County, CA's board-ordered pause is now itself tangled in litigation alongside a separate water-rights suit from the developer.

  • Recurring resident concerns nationwide: 24/7 industrial noise, strain on water supplies, higher electricity bills, property values, and loss of rural/agricultural land.
  • Local governments are increasingly using town halls and dedicated community meetings (Charlotte, NC; Grady County, OK) as a pressure-release valve before any zoning vote.
  • Congress remains gridlocked: a House Energy & Commerce data-center bill cleared committee 52-0 in July but GOP leadership has not scheduled a floor vote as of Sept. 4.
