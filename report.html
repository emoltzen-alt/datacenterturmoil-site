Today's national picture. Local resistance to data centers keeps widening rather than narrowing: at least 55 U.S. counties have now enacted some form of moratorium, and Data Center Watch estimates roughly $130 billion in projects were blocked or delayed nationwide just in Q1 2026. Water use, spiking electricity bills, noise from cooling systems and backup generators, and loss of local control over zoning remain the near-universal grievances, cutting across red and blue jurisdictions alike.

Genuinely new in the last 24 hours: Because July 11–12 fell on a weekend, formal council/commission votes were scarce, but grassroots activity was not — dozens of residents from across central Virginia (Hanover, Henrico, Louisa, Botetourt, Hampton Roads) rallied at the State Capitol in Richmond on Saturday, July 11, demanding a permitting pause, an end to NDA-shrouded rezonings, and citing new FOIA figures showing some data centers using 11–12 million gallons of water a day alongside a 25% Dominion Energy rate hike that took effect July 1. Separately, Austin County, Texas officials announced (in reporting published July 11) a public hearing on a proposed moratorium covering both data centers and battery energy storage systems. Most other items below reflect the wave of moratorium votes and lawsuits from the preceding week, carried here as background.

Richmond, Virginia Current
Capitol Square rally · statewide grassroots · Saturday, July 11, 2026

Dozens of Virginians from Hanover, Henrico, Louisa, Botetourt and Hampton Roads rallied at the Capitol Square Bell Tower demanding a pause on data center permits, full transparency, an end to "backroom" rezonings and NDAs, and community input before any permits are granted. Speakers cited FOIA data showing some facilities drawing 11–12 million gallons of water a day and a 25% Dominion Energy rate increase effective July 1.

Source: WTVR — "Protesters rally at Capitol over data center concerns" · 12 On Your Side, July 12, 2026
Austin County, Texas Current
County Commissioners · public hearing announced · reported July 11, 2026

County officials announced a public hearing (at the Austin County Expo Center in Bellville) on a proposed moratorium covering both data centers and battery energy storage systems (BESS), saying existing review processes aren't sufficient and a health-and-safety review is needed before any such construction in unincorporated areas.

Source: KWHI.com — "Austin Co. to hold public hearing Monday on proposed moratorium"

Sarasota County, Florida Older
County Commission · vote 5–0 · Wednesday, July 8, 2026

Commissioners unanimously blocked acceptance, review or approval of hyperscale data center applications for at least a year, giving staff time to evaluate how the projects fit the zoning code. Follow-up coverage two days later reiterated county leaders' "no, not now, never" stance.

Source: WGCU — "Sarasota County blocks hyperscale data centers for at least one year" · Your Observer, July 11, 2026
Missoula County, Montana Older
Board of County Commissioners · unanimous vote · Thursday, July 9, 2026

Commissioners adopted interim zoning pausing new or expanding data centers for up to a year — the first such moratorium in Montana — after 30 members of the public spoke, all in favor, citing public-health, water and grid concerns.

Source: Daily Inter Lake — "Missoula County adopts temporary moratorium on data centers"
Douglas County, Georgia Older
County Commission · vote 4–1 to deny · early July 2026

Commissioners rejected a rezoning request for a proposed 700-acre, five-building, 4.4-million-square-foot data center campus near I-20, after a packed hearing where opponents brought sound clips demonstrating cooling-system noise. It was the second time in seven months the county turned down large-scale data center plans on the site.

Source: FOX 5 Atlanta — "Douglas County leaders just killed a massive data center project"
Terrell County, Georgia Older
Board of Commissioners · unanimous vote · Tuesday, July 7, 2026

Commissioners adopted a six-month moratorium on new data centers at the Terrell County Courthouse in Dawson, following the lead of neighboring Lee County, to give officials time to draft siting and operating rules.

Source: Albany Herald — "Following Lee's lead, Terrell County hits pause on data centers"
Garfield Township (Grand Traverse County), Michigan Older
Planning Commission · recommendation vote · Wednesday, July 8, 2026

Planners recommended a zoning amendment letting the township board impose a temporary data center moratorium (up to 18 months) after a crowd of hundreds packed the meeting room, spilling into the lobby. Nearly 40 residents spoke and none opposed the pause; several, citing water-use worries, wanted an outright ban instead.

Source: 9&10 News — "Garfield Township planners recommend moratorium power aimed at data centers"
