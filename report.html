Today's national picture. Local resistance to data centers keeps broadening and hardening: opposition groups have more than doubled since late 2025 to over 800 across 49 states, a Gallup poll finds roughly 7 in 10 Americans oppose a data center near them, and analysts count nearly $130 billion in projects blocked or delayed so far this year — the fastest pace since tracking began. Water, electricity rates, noise, and loss of farmland remain the dominant complaints, moratoriums remain the go-to municipal tool, and the fight is increasingly spilling into courts, utility disputes, and state-level executive action, not just zoning boards. The issue is also now visibly shaping midterm primary campaigns in states such as Wisconsin, Ohio and Michigan.

Genuinely new in the last 24 hours: the Ohio Supreme Court unanimously ordered the village of Ashville to allow a resident referendum on a data-center/gas-plant deal (Aug. 7); Nevada's largest utility, NV Energy, sued data-center developer Tract in a first-of-its-kind case over who pays for AI-driven grid expansion, reported Aug. 7; Vanderburgh County, Indiana officials began a process to pause data-center applications until year's end while a study committee is formed (Aug. 7); a standing-room-only Meta data-center town hall turned tense in El Paso, Texas over water and power questions (Aug. 7); and fresh reporting from Wythe County, Virginia (Aug. 8) captured growing resident pushback — over farmland loss, water and electric rates, and closed-door NDAs — as two large campuses advance in a county with no zoning ordinance.

Ashville, Ohio Current
Ohio Supreme Court · ruling · August 7, 2026

The state's highest court unanimously ruled that the village of Ashville could not block a citizen referendum on its deal with EdgeConneX for two data centers and a gas plant by labeling it non-referendable "emergency" legislation, ordering officials to submit the petition to Pickaway County election officials. Residents had gathered signatures in May after opposing the deal.

Source: WOSU Public Media — "Ohio Supreme Court sides with Ashville residents trying to overturn data center agreement"
Storey County / Reno, Nevada Current
Utility lawsuit · Washoe County District Court · reported August 7, 2026

NV Energy sued data-center developer Tract in a first-of-its-kind case, alleging the company is trying to shift the cost of new grid capacity for its 2,025-megawatt Tahoe Reno Industrial Center campuses onto other ratepayers rather than paying for the buildout — a dispute with direct stakes for household electric bills statewide. Tract disputes the characterization, citing nearly $1 billion in committed infrastructure spending.

Source: CBS News — "Nevada energy company sues data center in first-of-its-kind fight over who should pay for AI buildout"
Vanderburgh County (Evansville), Indiana Current
Area Plan Commission · ordinance process · August 7, 2026

County officials began work on an ordinance to create a data-center research committee and pause acceptance, processing and approval of any data-center applications through Dec. 31, giving the county time to study land, water and utility impacts before applications are considered. Officials stressed the measure is not a ban.

Source: 14 News (WFIE) — "Vanderburgh officials begin process on data center committee"
El Paso, Texas Current
Community town hall · August 7, 2026

A standing-room-only town hall in Northeast El Paso turned tense as residents pressed city and utility officials — including El Paso Water's and El Paso Electric's chiefs — on the Meta data center campus's projected water and electricity demand, possible pollution, and whether costs could be passed on to other ratepayers.

Source: KFOX14 — "Meta data center town hall turns tense as residents press water, power officials"
