North Mankato, Minnesota Current
City Council · public hearing, moratorium tabled · August 3, 2026

Council chambers were full for a public hearing on a proposed 12-month moratorium on large-scale data centers (facilities over 250,000 sq ft or using 50,000+ gallons of water/day). Of roughly 15 speakers, only one opposed the moratorium itself — most others asked the council to tighten the thresholds (smaller size, lower water/power limits). The council tabled the ordinance to revise the language rather than vote it up or down.

Salem, Oregon Current
City Council · special meeting · August 3, 2026

The Loucks Auditorium at the downtown public library was at capacity by 5:45 p.m. ahead of a 6 p.m. special council meeting on a possible data center moratorium. After hearing extensive public testimony against proposed projects, councilors voted 8–0 to instead create a nine-member Data Center Task Force to research impacts on public resources, hold town halls, and bring recommendations back by year's end — stopping short of a moratorium.

Source: Salem Reporter — "Salem City Council hears public testimony on proposed data center moratorium" · Salem Business Journal (Aug. 3, 2026)
Farmville, North Carolina Current
Town Commissioners · moratorium adopted · Monday, August 3, 2026

Commissioners voted unanimously to adopt a 12-month moratorium on data centers after residents cited environmental impacts, water usage, and potential utility-rate increases at a public hearing during the board's regular meeting. The town manager said no data center has actually been proposed or approved, but a renewable-energy company had pitched the concept to town leaders earlier this year; the moratorium buys time to write standards.

Source: WITN — "Farmville board adopts 12-month moratorium on potential data center after residents voice opposition" (Aug. 4, 2026)
Menomonie, Wisconsin Current
City Council · draft ordinance introduced (no vote) · August 3, 2026

After months of public outcry over the secrecy and scale of a proposed 320-acre data center campus, the city council introduced — without voting on — a draft "I-4" zoning ordinance establishing development standards for building height, setbacks, light and noise pollution, water use, landscaping, and site reclamation.

Source: Wisconsin Public Radio — "After public outcry, Menomonie will release draft guidelines for data center project"
Genoa, Ohio Current

Residents again brought concerns to the village council over a proposed data center redevelopment south of State Road 163 — a debate stretching back three years — citing possible declines in home values, reduced water availability, and noise and light pollution. No new vote was taken; the council continues working to address resident concerns.
