National Trends

Local pushback on data center development continued at pace across the country. In the past day, county and city boards in Arizona, Virginia, California, Iowa, Indiana and Pennsylvania all took up data center restrictions or contested hearings — two of them (Pima County, AZ and Prince William County, VA) landing major votes that curb or pause new development. The pattern seen over the past several weeks held: moratoriums (Oakland, Richmond, San Francisco, Baltimore County, North Port, Normal, Leavenworth County) and outright rejections (Loudoun County, DeKalb County, Kansas City, Allen Park) are increasingly common, alongside a growing wave of resident and environmental-group lawsuits (Effingham County/OpenAI, Farmington UT, Washington County OR) challenging how approvals were granted. Recurring resident concerns are consistent nationwide: strain on water and electricity, noise from cooling systems and gas-fired backup power, property values, loss of farmland/green space, and generous local tax breaks granted with limited public input.
