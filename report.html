National Trends

Local opposition to data center development continues to accelerate nationwide, now compressing into a faster boom-to-backlash cycle than the 2010s fracking wave, according to an NPR interview with environmentalist Bill McKibben published Sept. 13. An August University of Pennsylvania poll found 61% of Americans oppose new data centers in their communities, up 12 points from earlier in the year, and a leaked National Republican Senatorial Committee memo reportedly flagged data centers as a "sleeper issue" for the 2026 midterms.

  • Moratoriums are multiplying fast: active or imminent pause votes this week alone in San Francisco (Sept. 15), Charlotte (extension discussed Sept. 14) and Gallatin, TN (formal vote expected this week), on top of recent pauses in Fort Worth, Indianapolis/Marion County, Old Town (ME), and Nottingham (NH).
  • Ballot-box fights: 18 Ohio communities will vote on data-center-related charter and zoning measures Nov. 3; Johnstown, OH set a January 2027 special election after missing that deadline. Wisconsin's Port Washington and California's Monterey Park recently passed anti-data-center referenda by wide margins.
  • Litigation is now a standard tactic on both sides — developers suing cities/townships over blocked or delayed projects (Prichard, AL; Cave City, KY; Monroe Township, NJ) and residents suing cities over approvals (Farmington; Independence). Data Center Watch estimates roughly $64B in U.S. data center projects have been blocked or delayed by local opposition over the past two years.
  • Core resident grievances are consistent: rising electricity bills, water use/well and pressure impacts, constant low-frequency noise, and a sense that public hearings move too fast for meaningful input (e.g., Mona, UT's four-minute hearing).
